The most important finding is that **the originating concept is not imaginary**. In September 2026 there are live senior roles explicitly responsible for analytical-production standards, production readiness, system integrity, data/quantitative components, controlled change, remediation, evidence, operating models, and accountability. BlackRock is recruiting a Director to establish and lead an **Analytics Production Readiness & Assurance** function; Bank of America is recruiting a Director for a regulatory risk/P&L platform with explicit **end-to-end BAU ownership and accountability across technology, data, and quantitative components**; AstraZeneca calls an Executive Director the **“overall accountable leader” for strategy and execution of quantitative analytics** across an infectious-disease clinical-development portfolio. citeturn17search2turn15search3turn15search1

But the evidence does **not** show an established, standardized occupation whose normal enterprise title is “Analytical Production Owner.” The market is better described as a function that has already appeared inside several established job families: Head of Data & Analytics, analytics-production/readiness leadership, quantitative-platform leadership, biometrics/statistical-programming leadership, data-platform leadership, CDAO/CDO leadership, model-risk/model-development leadership, and analytics engineering. Deloitte's 2026 CDAO survey of 100 C-suite executives at companies with at least $1 billion in revenue found 63% of respondents described themselves as primary drivers of data-and-analytics decisions and 89% said their CDAO role could actively evolve or transform data/AI strategy with full executive support, confirming a substantial executive market around the broader function even though that market is not synonymous with analytical-production ownership. citeturn11view0

### Market classification

The best-supported classification is therefore:

**B. Emerging / Fragmented Role — High confidence.** The function is plainly present, but responsibilities are distributed among several market-recognized titles.

**C. Sector-Specific Role — High confidence.** The closest matches occur where analytical outputs are operationally consequential, technically complex, recurring, regulated, client-facing, or subject to formal quality expectations—especially financial risk and capital markets, pharmaceutical/clinical analytics, and other high-consequence production environments. citeturn17search2turn15search3turn15search1turn22search3

**D. Consulting / Advisory Opportunity — High confidence.** Major firms sell operating-model design, data/AI governance, model-lifecycle governance, analytics modernization, trusted-data programs, controls, and assessment/remediation services that collectively cover much of the proposed function. citeturn10search0turn19search0turn19search1turn19search7

**A. Established Role — only at narrower boundaries, Medium confidence.** “System owner,” “product owner,” “Head of Data,” “Head of Biometrics,” and “Head of Data Platform” are established concepts. What is not established is a universal enterprise role spanning every platform, analytical method, data domain, validation activity, and downstream business use. citeturn20search0turn12search5turn14search0

The evidence does **not** support classification E, Weak Market Viability, for the underlying function. It **does** support a weak-market warning for trying to commercialize the function under an unfamiliar abstract title without translating it into vocabulary buyers already use.

### What the pattern says about organization size

The evidence indicates a fairly clear size effect.

In **smaller or growth-stage organizations**, “Head of Data,” “Head of Data & Analytics,” or a similar leader can plausibly own most of the stack because the system boundary is relatively compact: data engineering, platform, analytics engineering, analytics/data science, metrics, governance and enablement can report to one person. Vanta, Bitvavo, PermitFlow and similar live postings illustrate this. citeturn9search0turn9search7turn9search4

In a **large or multinational enterprise**, the same idea survives, but the economically meaningful boundary usually becomes a specific production estate, analytical domain, portfolio, therapeutic area, risk system, or platform. BlackRock defines APRA inside Analytics Production Delivery; Bank of America defines SFRC as a strategic risk/P&L system; AstraZeneca defines accountable biometrics leadership by therapeutic portfolio; ENGIE defines one leader for the enterprise-grade platform serving Trading, Risk, Analytics and Quant. **This is evidence for narrower systems, not evidence against one owner per system.** citeturn17search2turn15search3turn15search1turn14search0

ENGIE is particularly instructive. Its Head of Data Platform has end-to-end ownership of the platform, including production-grade operation, Tier-0 reliability, security, AI/ML infrastructure, governance and platform adoption, but the posting explicitly says **business domains retain ownership of their data**. That is exactly the category distinction posed in this study: the existence of separately owned source/domain systems does not create co-ownership of the platform. **Evidence class: observed organizational practice from current job-market evidence.** citeturn14search0

## Ownership, authority, and the falsification test

### Singular ownership is a mainstream operating concept

Singular ownership is not unique to analytics.

NIST defines a **system owner** as the organizational official responsible for the overall procurement, development, integration, modification, operation, and maintenance of a system. NIST's Risk Management Framework also deliberately assigns different outcomes to different roles; its authorization step requires a senior official to determine whether the risk of operating a system is acceptable. **Evidence class: supervisory/professional guidance.** citeturn20search0turn20search11

Scrum supplies an especially clean management analogue. The official Scrum Guide makes the Product Owner accountable for product value and effective backlog management, allows work to be delegated without delegating away accountability, and explicitly specifies that the Product Owner is **one person, not a committee**. When multiple Scrum teams work on one product, the guide says they should share the product goal, backlog, and Product Owner. This is management-framework evidence rather than proof about analytics, but it strongly establishes that modern operating practice does not regard singular accountable ownership as antiquated or inherently incompatible with collaborative work. **Evidence class: leadership/management theory and professional practice.** citeturn12search5turn12search4

The current job market then supplies the empirical bridge: Bank of America calls for ownership across multiple contributing teams; BlackRock expects one Director to establish an operating capability that holds upstream and downstream participants to standards; ENGIE expects one Head to own an end-to-end platform while collaborating with independently accountable domains. **Evidence class: current job-market evidence + synthesis.** citeturn15search3turn17search2turn14search0

The practical conclusion is:

> **Delegation, multiple specialist teams, external inputs, independent validation, and matrixed reporting do not by themselves defeat singular system ownership. The test is whether one leader remains answerable for the bounded system's acceptable operation and possesses sufficient decision rights to correct it.**

### The authority test

The strongest market matches possess much more than a title.

A credible analytical-production owner needs effective authority to establish or enforce production standards; control production acceptance or release through direct authority or an enforceable gate; require remediation; prioritize corrective work; obtain or allocate resources; manage incidents; preserve evidence; escalate unresolved risk; and verify that remediation actually reaches production.

Bank of America's SFRC role satisfies much of this test explicitly: end-to-end BAU ownership, production stability, incident/remediation leadership, controlled release/change, prioritization, resource allocation, platform financials, audit/regulatory response, and responsibility for ensuring changes become measurable production improvements. citeturn15search3turn15search4

BlackRock satisfies the standards, readiness, measurement, accountability, evidence and remediation-decision portions particularly strongly, although the public posting does not establish an unrestricted unilateral stop-work or release veto. citeturn17search2

AstraZeneca's Biometrics Head clearly satisfies organizational answerability for quantitative strategy and execution across its stated domain, while the public posting does not show ownership of every underlying infrastructure platform. citeturn15search1

This produces a useful distinction:

**A person who owns only standards is governance. A person who owns only infrastructure is a platform owner. A person who owns only methods is an analytical or scientific leader. A genuine analytical-production owner must be able to cause the bounded production system to become acceptable again when it fails.**

That final sentence is **synthesis**, supported by the observed authority patterns above and consistent with LR-024's original intervention-oriented definition of accountability. fileciteturn1file0

### Sarbanes-Oxley supports category discipline, not a simplistic “one CFO” analogy

Sarbanes-Oxley is useful, but it does **not** prove the literal proposition that financial reporting always has one human owner. Section 302 requires both the principal executive officer and principal financial officer to certify periodic reports and assigns those officers responsibility for establishing, maintaining and evaluating relevant internal controls. **Evidence class: regulatory/legal responsibility.** citeturn21search0

More important for this study is the separation between **production and assurance**. PCAOB standards say management is responsible for the financial statements and for maintaining and assessing internal control over financial reporting, while the independent auditor is responsible for expressing an opinion. The auditor's responsibility does not transform the auditor into a co-owner of management's financial-reporting production system. **Evidence class: regulatory/professional guidance.** citeturn21search2turn21search12

Therefore the strongest lesson from SOX/PCAOB is not “there must always be exactly one person.” It is:

> **Distinct systems and distinct outcomes receive identifiable accountability. Independent assurance remains independent of the operating outcome it examines.**

That materially supports the proposed **interface principle** without improperly converting legal certification into evidence of singular operational ownership.

### Banking model-risk practice reaches the same boundary

The agencies' April 17, 2026 revised model-risk guidance explicitly separates model development/use, validation/monitoring, and governance/controls, while emphasizing clear roles and responsibilities and risk-based tailoring. It is most relevant to banking organizations above $30 billion in assets but is deliberately nonprescriptive. **Evidence class: supervisory guidance.** citeturn22search0turn22search3turn22search6

This is not a counterexample to one-owner-per-system. Model construction/use, independent validation, internal audit, and the business decision that consumes the model output are deliberately different functions with different outcomes. The guidance's existence does, however, show why a proposed analytical-production system boundary cannot casually absorb independent validation or audit merely to make the organizational chart look simpler. **Evidence class: supervisory guidance + synthesis.** citeturn22search3turn22search10

### Genuine counterexamples

**No genuine counterexample identified.**

The search found many examples of plural responsibilities, but none that satisfied the study's complete eight-part falsification test for two or more non-subordinate people intentionally recognized as co-equal owners of **the same whole operating system and the same whole-system outcome**.

The important near-misses are:

| Apparent counterexample | Why it fails the strict test |
|---|---|
| CEO + CFO SOX certifications | Joint statutory/certification responsibilities; not evidence that both are co-equal operational owners of one production system. citeturn21search0 |
| Management + external auditor | Explicitly distinct outcomes: management prepares/controls; auditor independently expresses an opinion. citeturn21search2turn21search12 |
| Model developer + validator + internal audit | Different lifecycle and assurance functions; validation deliberately challenges production rather than co-owning it. citeturn22search3turn22search10 |
| Federal CDO + Evaluation Officer + Statistical Official | The Evidence Act created three distinct senior functions intended to coordinate; it did not make all three co-owners of one analytical production system. citeturn12search13 |
| ENGIE platform + business-domain data owners | Explicitly separate system boundaries: Head owns the platform; business domains retain their own data ownership. citeturn14search0 |
| BlackRock APRA + Analytics Stewards + Product + modelers | Different responsibilities around production readiness, domain standards, product and model supply; public evidence does not say they are co-equal owners of the same whole system. citeturn17search2 |

Absence of a discovered counterexample is not mathematical proof that none exists. It does mean that **plural ownership should not be treated as the presumptive organizational model without actual evidence**, because the most relevant evidence reviewed instead shows either singular ownership, delegated work, separate subsystems, or production/assurance separation.

### Why responsibilities are fragmented where they are fragmented

The evidence does not support a single explanation. At least four different phenomena are present.

**Some “fragmentation” is really multiple systems.** Source-data production, analytical production, independent validation, audit and final business decision each have distinct outputs. SOX/PCAOB and banking model-risk practice strongly demonstrate this. citeturn21search2turn22search3

**Some division is intentionally necessary for independence.** An operating owner cannot also supply credible independent assurance of the same work. Current banking guidance retains validation, monitoring, governance and controls as distinguishable functions, and PCAOB standards require an independent auditor rather than turning audit into another production team. citeturn22search3turn21search2

**Some fragmentation reflects scale.** Large firms do not normally collapse all analytics into one manageable system boundary. Their postings create owners around concrete estates—Aladdin analytics-production readiness, SFRC risk/P&L, infectious-disease biometrics, or EEMNA's data/AI platform. Smaller companies can economically give a Head of Data much broader scope. citeturn17search2turn15search3turn15search1turn9search0

**And some fragmentation appears to be market immaturity rather than architectural necessity.** Deloitte's 2026 CDAO survey shows senior data leaders gaining authority while still reporting shortages of resources, skills and mature guardrails; meanwhile firms such as LaunchDarkly create new roles specifically because critical metrics previously crossed several teams without one accountable owner. **Evidence class: empirical/practitioner evidence + current job-market evidence.** citeturn11view0turn13search12

That last category is commercially important. It means a consulting or transformation engagement may have value precisely where the organization has not yet decided what the permanent ownership boundary should be.

### Consulting-market evidence

The external-services market is unambiguous even though it does not usually use the label “analytical production ownership.”

KPMG markets help for CDAOs that explicitly combines **operating model, clear ownership, decision rights and accountability, governance, certified data pipelines, lineage, policy controls and modern architecture**. Its Modern Data Platform offering combines business-aligned data/analytics vision, trust in data and reporting, analytical tools, processes and governance. Its actuarial practice sells model development/remediation, process automation, assumption governance and target-operating-model review. **Evidence class: current consulting-market evidence.** citeturn19search0turn19search7turn19search11

PwC's Model Edge product/service proposition covers model inventory, configurable lifecycle workflows, governance, policy-aligned controls, compliance tracking and audit trails; its data-risk practice explicitly offers end-to-end data-risk frameworks, governance, quality, incident response and AI governance. **Evidence class: current consulting-market evidence.** citeturn10search0turn10search15

Deloitte markets advisory, implementation and managed services for Data & Digital Trust, including data-governance strategy, risk assessment, program design, implementation and operations; its public examples include establishing operating models that assign product ownership and monitor changing requirements. citeturn19search1

Accenture describes its 2026 Databricks work as helping organizations unify data foundations, centralize governance, move AI from experimentation into production and scale production-ready analytics/AI applications. citeturn10search2

The conclusion is straightforward: **consulting firms are already selling substantial pieces of the proposed ownership function.** What clients commonly buy is an assessment, operating model, modernization, control framework, implementation, remediation, or managed capability—not an outsider permanently accepting internal accountability for every analytical result.

This makes **transformation and advisory work commercially more legible than selling “outsourced accountability.”**

### Fractional leadership

There is also an identifiable fractional market. Current providers explicitly market Fractional Chief Data Officer or Fractional Chief Analytics Officer engagements around data strategy, analytics, governance, architecture, team leadership and accountable outcomes. Examples target healthcare/health-tech, mid-market organizations and growth-stage businesses. citeturn18search0turn18search4turn18search10

That evidence is predominantly provider self-description rather than independent market-size data, so the conclusion should remain restrained: **fractional analytical-production leadership is commercially plausible and visibly sold, but the evidence does not establish a large mature market comparable to permanent CDAO/data leadership or mainstream consulting.** **Evidence class: practitioner/vendor evidence + inference.**

### The viable role architecture

If an organization chooses to create a true analytical-production ownership role, the evidence supports the following boundary.

**Outcome owned:** reliable, fit-for-declared-purpose analytical evidence produced and released through the defined production system—not every downstream business decision and not the correctness of independently owned source systems.

**System owned:** the controlled path by which authorized analytical work is developed, tested, productionized, executed, accepted, released, monitored, corrected, and evidenced.

**The owner should directly own or possess enforceable decision rights over:** production standards; controlled execution/release; readiness criteria; production quality; configuration/change control; qualification requirements for controlled production activity; monitoring; analytical incidents; root-cause/remediation; production evidence; exceptions; and continuous improvement. This architecture closely matches authority visible in the BlackRock and Bank of America postings. citeturn17search2turn15search3

**Normally outside that ownership boundary:** creation of source-system data where another system owns it; general enterprise IAM/infrastructure where centrally provided; independent model validation; internal/external audit; legal/compliance determinations; independent assurance; and the downstream business decision. Current ENGIE, PCAOB and banking-model-risk evidence support keeping these interfaces distinct. citeturn14search0turn21search2turn22search3

A useful authority check is:

> **When an analytical-production failure occurs, can this leader require the system to stop, correct, retest, re-release, document the event, obtain the necessary resources, and demonstrate that the defect has actually been remediated?**

If the answer is no, the role is probably stewardship, coordination, governance, architecture, or advice rather than genuine operational ownership.

## Career implication and revised DR-015 conclusion

### What the market evidence means

For a highly experienced analytics-platform and analytical-production professional, **this is a legitimate career direction**, with an important condition: pursue the **function the market funds**, not necessarily the abstract title used in the research.

The strongest employment positioning is not:

> “I want to be the person accountable for all analytics.”

The market evidence favors something closer to:

> **“I lead the production system that turns analytical work into reliable organizational evidence—platforms, production standards, controlled change, quality, practitioner operating practices, release, remediation, auditability, and modernization.”**

That proposition maps directly onto responsibilities employers are currently paying Director, VP, Head, and Executive Director compensation for. BlackRock's $170K–$275K APRA range, ENGIE's $176.9K–$271.2K Head of Data Platform range, AstraZeneca's $168.8K–$253.2K Statistical Programming Director range, Vanta's $316K–$372K Head of Data & Analytics range, and the $321K–$520K range reported for LaunchDarkly's Head of Data & Analytics demonstrate that organizations attach substantial economic value to leadership positions containing major portions of the function. citeturn17search7turn14search0turn15search0turn9search0turn13search12

The evidence also gives a warning. A candidate who presents the concept as requiring hierarchical ownership of every data source, analyst, method specialist, infrastructure team, validator, auditor and business decision-maker will be fighting actual organizational practice. Current organizations instead give strong owners a **defined outcome and defined system**, then require them to work across interfaces with other genuinely owned systems. citeturn14search0turn21search2turn22search3

That does **not** require retreating into an assumption of “shared ownership.” It requires drawing the system boundary honestly.

### Revised DR-015 conclusion

The research supports the following answer:

> **One result. One system. Who owns it?**
>
> **For a genuinely defined analytical-production system, the evidence supports having one identifiable operational owner as a coherent and commercially credible model.**
>
> That owner may delegate extensively and may depend on separately owned data, infrastructure, validation, assurance and business-decision systems. Those interfaces do not, by themselves, create shared ownership of the analytical-production system.
>
> **The market for that function is real, but the title is not standardized.**

The strongest evidence does **not** justify claiming that every enterprise should have one person who owns all analytics. Large organizations commonly contain multiple analytical-production systems, and regulated organizations intentionally separate production from independent challenge and assurance. citeturn15search3turn15search1turn22search3turn21search2

Nor did the research produce a genuine falsifier in which two or more peers were intentionally recognized as co-equal owners of the same whole analytical operating system and the same whole-system outcome. The plural arrangements encountered were legal co-responsibility, separate lifecycle functions, independent assurance, separate systems, or delegated/contributing responsibilities.

The final market judgment is therefore:

**Operational proposition:** **Supported — High confidence.**

**Broad market proposition:** **Partly Supported — High confidence.**

**Best market characterization:** **Emerging/fragmented + sector-specific + strong consulting/transformation opportunity.**

**Permanent-employment sweet spot:** ownership of a **bounded, consequential analytical-production system or portfolio**, especially in finance, life sciences, regulated analytics, risk, enterprise data/AI platforms, and other environments where production failure has measurable consequences. citeturn17search2turn15search3turn15search1turn14search0

**Consulting sweet spot:** diagnose fragmented analytical production, define the operating boundary and accountable owner, modernize the platform and control system, establish production standards and evidence, remediate recurring failures, and leave the organization with a durable operating model. Major consulting firms are already selling adjacent components of exactly this problem. citeturn19search0turn10search0turn19search1turn10search2

**Fractional/interim sweet spot:** smaller and mid-market organizations that have outgrown informal analytics but cannot yet justify a permanent senior data/analytics executive; viable, but less mature as a market than transformation consulting or permanent leadership. citeturn18search4turn18search10

The research therefore **does not support stopping investment in the concept**. It supports refining the commercial proposition.

The proposition worth pursuing is not an invented universal governorship over enterprise analytics. It is much simpler:

> **A consequential analytical-production system needs somebody who can answer for whether it works, has the authority to make it work, and remains accountable when it does not.**
>
> **Organizations already fund that job. They just call it different things.**
